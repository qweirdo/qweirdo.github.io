Historical Epigraphy & Underground Ethos

The Graffiti History & Manifesto Archive

Explore the foundational concepts, philosophies, and historical epochs of graffiti. Click "Load Stencil" on any movement to place its authentic pochoir cutout onto the wall—then spray directly through the mask!

Classical Epigraphy 79 AD

Pompeii Graffito

"Admiror, O paries, te non cecidisse ruinis, qui tot scriptorum taedia sustineas."
— I marvel, O wall, that you have not collapsed under the weight of so many writers' scribblings.

Before printing presses or paper, the public wall was humanity's first unmediated open commons. Citizens carved poems, election slogans, and philosophical epigrams directly into Roman plaster.

The Moniker 1930s – 1940s

Bozo Texino & Kilroy

"Kilroy Was Here — The chalk mark that preceded every battalion."

Hobo rail workers and WWII soldiers marked existence against the crushing industrial grid. A chalked profile claiming presence where individual life was deemed anonymous and expendable.

Street Originators 1965 – 1971

Cornbread & TAKI 183

"Cornbread Loves Flora · TAKI 183 Five-Borough Messenger."

The birth of modern street writing in Philadelphia and NYC. Name + Street Number written in flowing chirography across transit corridors, proving that no municipality can render the individual invisible.

Typographic Revolution 1973 – 1975

Phase 2 & Stay High 149

"Bubble letters designed to be read as trains roar past platforms at 40 MPH."

Phase 2 invents 'Softies' (inflated bubble letters) to conquer transit velocity. Stay High 149 introduces the iconic Smokin' Stickman halo glyph, transforming handstyles into iconic street trademarks.

Gothic Futurism 1979

Rammellzee: Ikonoklast Panzerism

"The letters are armed to liberate themselves from bureaucratic dictionaries."

The military fortification of typography. Letters armed with 45-degree arrowheads, mechanical bevels, rocket thrusters, and 3D armor so municipal institutions can neither read nor commodify them.

The Subway Master Burner 1980 – 1983

Dondi White & Lady Pink

"Children of the Grave — Rolling museum canvases conquering the five boroughs."

Top-to-bottom whole cars on NYC transit steel. Lady Pink shatters gender barriers; Dondi pioneers intricate wildstyle architecture celebrated in Tony Silver's Style Wars and Martha Cooper's Subway Art.

Pochoir & The Commons 1980s – Present

Blek le Rat & The Right to the City

"A wall belongs to everyone. Advertising colonizes the gaze; street art decolonizes it."

Guerilla stencil cutouts reclaiming public space from unauthorized commercial advertising. If corporate billboards impose themselves on the public gaze without consent, the citizen holds an inalienable right to respond.

About Qweirdo

Qweirdo is a not-for-profit generative art installation and digital humanities experiment in slow, collective authorship.

The Palimpsest Wall (Digitally Aided Street Canvas). An authentic aerosol and street typography simulator grounded in graffiti history and manifestos. Features true nozzle particle dispersion (Fat, Skinny, Chisel Mop, Spatter), dynamic surface gravity drips, procedural substrates (1977 MTA Subway Car steel, 1973 IRT ceramic tiles, Bronx brick, wheatpaste billboards), and the MTA Buff Roller creating historical pentimento.

Algorithmic Wildstyle ("The Panzerist Engine"). Grounded in Rammellzee's 1979 Ikonoklast Panzerism manifesto and Phase 2's bubble letters, algorithmically fortifying typography with 45-degree arrowheads, 3D extruded drop blocks, chrome horizon fills, and power keylines.

The Resonance Canvas (Harmonograph Scriptorium). Rethinking the canvas as a physical resonance table. Coupled gravitational oscillators trace musical intervals into hypnotic Guilloché rosettes with live Web Audio chimes, vector SVG export, and live watermark imprinting.

Lumen Antiqua (Directional Illumination). Real-time directional candlelight casts warm specular caustics across gold leaf and embossed rims, mimicking traditional medieval illumination that reacts dynamically as light shifts across the page.

Scribe's Palimpsest Loupe. An interactive 2.5x magnifying lens equipped with an antique brass astrolabe bezel reveals microscopic rag paper fibers, capillary ink seeps, and gilded leaf craquelure.

Carta Manuscripta (Folio Press). Visitors can export high-resolution museum broadsides (3200×1600) complete with illuminated acanthus borders, Scribe Colophon, astronomical solar timestamps, and hot-wax embossed seals.
